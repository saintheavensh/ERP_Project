# Notification Triggers

## Purpose

This document defines the notification trigger framework used throughout the Universal Service ERP.

The framework establishes how business events generate notifications for users, customers, technicians, managers, and external systems.

Notifications ensure that important business activities receive timely attention and appropriate action.

---

# Overview

A notification trigger is a predefined condition that causes the system to generate a notification.

Triggers are typically generated from business events, workflow transitions, approvals, status changes, or exception conditions.

Examples:

* Service status changes
* Customer approval requests
* Inventory shortages
* Payment confirmations
* Warranty expirations

---

# Objectives

The Notification Trigger Framework is designed to:

* Improve operational awareness.
* Reduce process delays.
* Improve customer communication.
* Support workflow execution.
* Improve accountability.
* Enable proactive management.

---

# Notification Model

The notification process follows a standard pattern.

```text id="nt1"
Business Event
        ↓
Trigger Evaluation
        ↓
Notification Created
        ↓
Recipient Determined
        ↓
Notification Delivered
```

---

# Notification Components

## Trigger

A condition that initiates notification generation.

Example:

```text id="nt2"
Repair Completed
```

---

## Notification

The message generated by the system.

Example:

```text id="nt3"
Repair has been completed and is ready for quality control.
```

---

## Recipient

The user, customer, or role receiving the notification.

Examples:

* Customer
* Technician
* Cashier
* Manager
* Inventory Staff

---

## Delivery Channel

The mechanism used to deliver notifications.

Examples:

* In-App Notification
* Dashboard Alert
* Email
* SMS
* Messaging Platform

# Escalation Notifications

Escalations occur when expected actions are delayed.

Examples:

```text id="nt25"
Approval Pending Too Long
Repair Delayed
QC Overdue
Payment Overdue
```

Escalations should notify higher authority levels.

Example:

```text id="nt26"
Technician
        ↓
Supervisor
        ↓
Manager
```

---

# Notification Rules

## Rule 1

Notifications should be generated only for meaningful business events.

---

## Rule 2

Duplicate notifications should be avoided.

---

## Rule 3

Notification content should be clear and actionable.

---

## Rule 4

Recipients should be determined by roles and business context.

---

## Rule 5

Notification failures should be logged.

---

# Notification Logging

The system should record:

* Notification ID
* Trigger Event
* Recipient
* Delivery Channel
* Delivery Status
* Timestamp

Notification history supports troubleshooting and auditing.
